# Demand signals and proven demand

The method behind steps 2 and 3 of `market-research`: hear the problem from the people
who have it, then find who already earns money solving it. Rules marked *(field)* come
from a real report this skill produced; the rest are the method as designed and are
**anticipated** until a real project confirms or corrects them.

## Where people talk

Cover at least three kinds of places, one of them peer-to-peer:

| Kind | Examples | What it is good for |
|---|---|---|
| Peer communities | subreddits, forums, public groups, question-and-answer sites | the problem in the customer's own words, workarounds, what they tried |
| Reviews of what exists | app stores, software review sites, marketplace reviews | what paying users miss; read the 2 to 4 star ones first |
| Builders' trails | issue trackers of open tools, changelogs, "alternative to" pages | requests nobody has served, and why |
| Search behavior | autocomplete, "people also ask", keyword volume | how the problem is phrased and whether the phrasing grows |

- **Go where the customer is, not where builders are.** A founders' forum says what
  founders would build, not what the customer suffers.
- **Search in the regions' languages.** A problem absent in English can be loud in
  Spanish or Portuguese, and that is where a regional product starts.
- *(field)* **Review sites alone are not enough.** The first real report quoted only
  review sites and comparison pages: it heard what users of existing products miss and
  never heard the people who use nothing yet.

## How to search

Search the pain, not the product category. Patterns that find people in the middle of
the problem, combined with the customer's words and with `site:` for each community:

- "how do I …", "is there a tool / app for …", "alternative to <competitor>"
- "I'm tired of …", "why is it so hard to …", "does anyone else …"
- "I pay for …", "worth it?", "cancelled", "switched from … to …"
- "<workaround> template / spreadsheet / script" (a shared workaround is a product
  nobody has built yet)

## What to record

One row per item: the quote (exact, two lines at most) · link · date · place ·
engagement (votes, replies) · signal strength. No user names and no personal details:
the link is enough, and the report stays publishable.

## Signal strength, weakest to strongest

1. Agrees with someone else's complaint.
2. Describes the problem unprompted.
3. Describes a workaround they built or follow.
4. Spends money or regular hours on that workaround.
5. Asks for a tool, or asks whether one exists.
6. Says they switched products, or cancelled one, because of it.

Strength 4 and above is evidence of willingness to pay. Below that it is evidence that
the problem exists, nothing more.

## Counting honestly

- **Count independent authors, not posts.** One loud thread is one cluster.
- **Date everything.** Signals older than 24 months show the problem existed, not that
  it still does: something may have solved it since.
- **Look for the evidence against:** "solved by …", "not a real problem", products that
  tried and closed (and why). A report with no evidence against has not looked.
- **Stop at saturation:** when the last ten items add no new problem, the clusters are
  known. Before that, the sample is the first page of results.
- **Mind who is talking.** Communities over-represent enthusiasts and the unhappy, and
  some posts are seeded by vendors. A complaint is not a purchase.

## Integrity and access

- **Quote only what was opened in this session.** A search result's snippet is not the
  page. If the thread can't be opened, list it as "not opened" with its link and ask the
  user to open it and paste the text.
- **Respect blocks.** Several large communities refuse automated readers. Do not look
  for a way around: use what is openly readable, or what the user pastes.
- **Never invent a quote to fill a row.** "No data" with a way to measure it is a
  result; a plausible quote is a fabrication the whole report inherits.

## Proven demand: who already earns from this

A problem people complain about and nobody pays to solve is a risk. A problem with
paying competitors is validated: the question becomes what to do differently.

- **Look in three circles:** the same problem for the same customer; the same problem for
  another segment or region; the same customer's neighboring problem.
- **Substitutes count**, general-purpose tools above all. *(field)* A general assistant
  was the main substitute for a specialized product, and it gained the product's key
  feature within weeks of the report.
- **None found after years of complaints?** Ask why before celebrating: hard to reach,
  unwilling to pay, regulated, or solved well enough for free.

## Reading revenue and usage figures

Rank each figure by where it comes from:

| Reliability | Source | Label |
|---|---|---|
| High | audited accounts, public filings, regulators | FACT |
| Medium | what the company itself published (revenue, customers, installs band on its store page, pricing × disclosed customers) | FACT, as claimed by the company |
| Low | modeled estimates from app-intelligence providers (for example Sensor Tower or AppMagic) and web-traffic estimators | ESTIMATE |
| Lowest | proxies: review counts, job postings, ads that keep running for months | ESTIMATE, method shown |

For a modeled estimate, record **provider · date · country · store · period**, and:

- **They are models, not measurements.** The stores publish no sales figures; providers
  infer them from chart rankings. Give the figure as a range.
- **Gross or net.** One provider's revenue is net of store fees (its own methodology
  page, opened 2026-11-17); others publish gross spending. The store's share is 15 to
  30%, so two honest figures for one app can differ by that much. Write which one it is.
- **In-app purchases only.** Advertising income, payments on the company's own website
  and third-party stores are outside these figures: an ad-funded app looks poor and a
  web-billed one invisible.
- **Small apps are noise.** The provider itself calls its estimates "hardly reliable"
  under about USD 10,000 or 10,000 downloads a month. Most niches a new product enters
  live under that line: use the figure to tell "thousands" from "millions", no further.
- **Free tiers show less** (top charts, coarse bands). Say which tier a figure came
  from, and never fill what the tier hides.
- **Cross-check with a second source** of another kind (a company statement, the
  installs band on the store page), not with a second model of the same charts.

## From proven model to wedge

- **Adapt, don't clone.** Keep what the earning product proves (who pays, for what, how
  they find it) and change one variable: segment, region, language, price model or
  channel. Changing three at once tests nothing.
- **The bottom-up starts here:** paying users of the proven products × the share this
  wedge can reach × the price the offer will test. Every factor carries its label.
